Supergroup Bots: Keeping Your Telegram Community Safe and Organized

Telegram supergroups—groups that can host thousands of members—require active management to stay secure, spam-free, and well-organized. Bots that automate tasks like user verification, content moderation, and member screening are essential tools for admins. Among these, AcceptJoinRequestBot (@AcceptJoinRequestBot) stands out as a solution for automating entry through a small verification system. This article compares it with four other bots in the same category: @antida2bot, @ChatSizeBot, @EmojiCaptchaBot, and @PBlacklistBot.

What Supergroup Bots Do

Supergroup bots handle repetitive admin tasks, such as: - Verifying new members (captchas, questions, or manual approval) - Removing inactive or deleted accounts - Calculating group statistics (file sizes, member counts) - Synchronizing bans across multiple groups - Preventing spam with custom rules

These bots reduce manual workload and enhance security, but each has a specific focus.

How AcceptJoinRequestBot Compares

AcceptJoinRequestBot is designed for groups with join requests enabled. Instead of manually approving each user, it can automatically accept or reject based on a simple verification step. This is ideal for groups that want a low-friction entry process without exposing the group to spam.

In contrast: - @antida2bot focuses on deleted account removal every 6 hours—useful for keeping member lists clean, but not for entry verification. - @ChatSizeBot calculates total file sizes in groups/channels—a niche utility, but unrelated to joining. - @EmojiCaptchaBot uses emoji captchas to verify humans—a more interactive, spam-proof method than AcceptJoinRequestBot’s simple verification. - @PBlacklistBot syncs bans across multiple groups—great for cross-group moderation, not entry.

Key Strengths of AcceptJoinRequestBot

  • Simplicity: Minimal setup—just enable join requests and add the bot.
  • Automation: Reduces admin time by handling approvals automatically.
  • Low barrier: Users only need to complete a small verification (e.g., clicking a button or answering a question).
  • No captcha fatigue: Less intrusive than image or emoji captchas, which some users find annoying.
