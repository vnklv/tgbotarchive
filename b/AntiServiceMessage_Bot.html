A Cleaner’s Guide to Telegram Join Bots: AntiServiceMessage_Bot vs. the Competition

Telegram groups and channels often generate clutter in the form of service messages—those automated notifications saying “User joined,” “User left,” or “User was added by admin.” While these messages can be useful for moderation, they quickly overwhelm active chats. A category of bots has emerged to handle this, but their approaches vary widely. This article compares @AntiServiceMessage_Bot with five similar bots in the “join” category, helping you choose the right tool for your needs.

Overview of Join Bots

Join bots primarily manage membership events in Telegram groups and channels. Their core functions include: - Removing service messages (join/leave/add notifications) - Automating join request approvals (for private groups/channels) - Filtering members based on criteria like Premium status

While some bots focus purely on cleanup, others add complex approval workflows or selective rejection features. The best choice depends on whether you need simple message removal or advanced membership control.

How AntiServiceMessage_Bot Stands Out

@AntiServiceMessage_Bot is laser-focused on one task: removing join, add, and leave messages in groups and supergroups. Unlike many competitors, it doesn’t handle join requests, member filtering, or posting tools. This simplicity is both its strength and its limitation.

Where other bots like @ChatReqBot or @PremiumKickBot add layers of functionality (request queues, Premium user rejection), AntiServiceMessage_Bot stays true to its name—it silently deletes service messages without additional configuration. For users who just want a clean chat history without extra features, this is ideal.

Key Strengths and Unique Features

  • Zero-configuration cleanup: Add the bot as an admin, and it immediately starts removing service messages. No commands or setup required.
  • Supergroup support: Works in both regular groups and supergroups, which is not guaranteed with all cleanup bots.
  • No extra permissions needed: Unlike bots that manage join requests, AntiServiceMessage_Bot only needs “Delete messages” rights, minimizing security risks.
  • Lightweight and reliable: With no voting systems or complex logic, it’s less prone to bugs or downtime.
